# Shared contracts

## Canonical record

Every article has one `article_id` and one canonical source. The package manifest records `canonical_sha256`; a reader-visible change to canonical content or visual payload creates a new fingerprint and re-enters its WQ author-QA or WR independent-review route before G. A public-page observation alone follows the public reader-page contract below and does not reopen the article route. `context/article-contract.json` and `reviews/review-index.json` are compact, hash-bound navigation records; neither can alter canonical content, owner scope or a recorded quality verdict.

## Role contract

| Role | May do | Must not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W | Research, draft and repair canonical content; in WQ, perform same-context adversarial self-QA and issue `AUTHOR_QA_READY` only after final hash binding | Present self-QA as independent R approval or decide release |
| R | Independently inspect and issue findings in WR only | Edit the article or change scope |
| G | Collect and report the pre-write plan, freeze the owner contract after confirmation, dispatch, decide contract acceptance and update state; resolve a recorded topic-evidence exception only inside its frozen slot | Draft text, claim W research is complete, re-review article quality, silently change a frozen topic or publish |
| Human | Use a native platform UI, accept or flag the reader page, and save the URL/state return receipt | Change canonical content without a correction record |

## Orchestration contract

### Pre-write owner-confirmation contract

Before any article artifact path, W, R, article queue, outline, prose, image, canonical package, or article-research subagent exists, campaign G maintains canonical `prewrite-plan.json` and runs `harnessctl.py sync-prewrite-plan` to render the owner-facing `prewrite-plan.md`. In current schema-2.14 workspaces the manifest is schema `1.8`; the Markdown report is a deterministic read-only view, never a separately hand-maintained plan. It has one shared `campaign_strategy` plus one card for every configured `article_id`: `task_and_audience`, `reader_value_and_secondary_cta`, `editorial_brief`, and `risks_and_owner_decisions`. Each card also shows a `frozen_delivery_mapping`, `topic_slot`, `evidence_posture`, and `review_effort`. `topic_slot` freezes the reader task, core intent, market, differentiation angle and prohibited deviations, but not literal keywords or the final title. The editorial brief consolidates the proposed reader problem, keyword/localization route, fact/source boundary, title/outline, visual narrative and known transport assumptions. Default `review_effort` is `AUTHOR_QA_INTEGRATED / WQ_SHARED_CONTEXT`; high-risk or owner-required independent review is `INDEPENDENT_R_ESCALATION / WR_INDEPENDENT`. It may cite an actual campaign shared-evidence pack, but never creates an empty cache merely to satisfy the plan. Schema-2.13 and older plans retain historical interpretation: their `STANDARD_INTEGRATED_REVIEW` always required independent R.

The owner must explicitly return `OWNER_PREWRITE_PLAN_CONFIRMED`. G first preserves the owner-originated receipt under `evidence/owner-confirmations/`, then uses `confirm-prewrite-plan` to bind the exact article IDs, confirmation ID, report SHA-256, manifest SHA-256, protected scope snapshot/hash, receipt path/hash, locator and timezone-aware timestamp in state, confirmation, requirements contract, and scope lock. A manually edited status or ID alone cannot promote the plan. The protected snapshot includes each article's language, market, platform, account, audience-fit mode, cross-language exception, `topic_slot`, and evidence posture. If any of these changes, `sync-prewrite-plan` returns `SCOPE_RECONFIRM_REQUIRED` without overwriting the old receipt; an explicit invalidation preserves the old record and returns the plan to owner confirmation. Before editorial dispatch, every article also needs the owner-confirmed one-to-one `{platform, account}` assignment and compatible audience/transport row. `OWNER_PREWRITE_PLAN_CHANGES_REQUESTED` never permits a partial dispatch. The dossier is a plan and known-evidence record, not `RESEARCH_READY` or `RESEARCH_APPROVED`: after confirmation W still conducts integrated research. WQ checks that research during its same-context adversarial self-QA; WR requires independent R `FULL_REVIEW`, optionally with a separately approved early research challenge if the risk warrants it.

### Article contract, review index, and delta capsule

At dispatch, the campaign G/harness writes one immutable `context/article-contract.json` for each article. It projects only the confirmed article-relevant scope: article ID, task/language/market, applicable `REQ-*`, reader-value/required CTA declaration, release-pair and locale references when applicable, plus paths and hashes for its confirmed campaign records. It deliberately excludes mutable open findings. It is derived from the global scope lock and cannot add, remove or reinterpret a requirement; this mechanical projection is not an extra article-G turn.

The active quality role maintains `reviews/review-index.json`: W for WQ, R for WR. It indexes the frozen `review_effort`, route resolution, latest decisions, current artifact hashes, receipt/report paths, mutable open/resolved/superseded stable finding IDs and next required input. Unescalated WQ records `latest_author_qa.status = AUTHOR_QA_READY`, a visible `ARTICLE_WRITER` / `AUTHOR_QA` task, a hash-bound short receipt, and `latest_full_review = NOT_REQUIRED`; its research-review coverage is `INTEGRATED_IN_AUTHOR_QA`. WR instead records an independent `latest_full_review = APPROVED` with visible R identity; separate early research approval is only a recorded WR option. WQ may only move one way to `WR_INDEPENDENT_ESCALATED`, recording trigger IDs; after that full independent R is mandatory. Ordinary turns load the contract, index and current changed artifacts, not all historic reports. After context compression reread [工作流核心](workflow-core.md), then these compact records. Full history is warranted only for hash drift, ambiguous finding lineage, agent replacement, scope conflict or explicit escalation. Navigation records never overrule primary evidence.

Only WR permits R-Δ. It begins with `reviews/review-delta-N.json`, naming the approved independent baseline hashes/report, exact changed paths, affected claims, `REQ-*` and stable finding IDs. R reads that capsule, changed artifacts and direct dependencies. Missing or contradictory data, hash drift, ambiguous finding lineage, a changed reader-value promise, or a scope change escalates to full R. After a final full review, `R_VISUAL_DELTA` remains limited to visual assets, manifest, payload or package visual pointer and binds final hashes. WQ changes instead rerun W's author-QA and refresh its final receipt; an author self-check cannot masquerade as R-Δ.

### Current artifact-source contract

For current schema-2.14, each `article-package.json@1.5` lives in `articles/<article_id>/` and is a one-way source index: `canonical/article.html`, `canonical/metadata.json`, `research/evidence-pack.json` and `canonical/visual-manifest.json`, plus frozen reader-value/CTA declarations. The compiler consumes exactly those package-declared paths and hashes; it cannot accept a second body, title, metadata or visual source. Evidence pack schema `1.3` records `topic_slot_alignment`, `reader_intent_basis`, bounded platform-profile use, evidence posture and actual shared-evidence citations; these structural checks do not score prose. `TOPIC_EVIDENCE_CONFLICT` blocks quality readiness until the same registered G records a bounded `KEEP_SLOT`/`NARROW_WITHIN_SLOT` decision with reason and evidence; a changed reader task, market or promise needs renewed owner confirmation. W compiles paired visual payloads before WQ final QA or WR full review: HTML is the rich-text transfer and primary semantic surface, Markdown the mechanically derived readable counterpart. Only `COMPANION_DUAL_READ_REQUIRED` requires semantic dual reading. WQ's `latest_author_qa` binds the candidate canonical hash, the final evidence pack, canonical, metadata, visual manifest, HTML/Markdown payload and article-package hashes to its short receipt and visible W identity; WR's `latest_full_review` binds those final artifacts to independent R. Every image has one ordered canonical-body marker, manifest ordinal, visible placement anchor and hash-matched numbered PNG/JPG/JPEG asset; aggregate image slots and WebP/GIF/SVG handoff assets remain invalid. Post-quality handoff finalization may only emit a manifest from byte-identical re-renders; a changed candidate first returns to the article's WQ or WR quality route. The manifest is derived once and G checks its chain plus `REQ-*` traceability, never silently overwriting a reviewed payload. Schema 2.13 / package 1.5 retains its historical independent-R contract; schema 2.12 / package 1.4 and older also retain their historical compatibility contracts.

The campaign shared-evidence pack is immutable within one confirmed content project and only contains real, bounded, reusable observations. A receiving article cites it only with exact key, current hash and record IDs; W records its local decision and freshness check, while the applicable WQ/WR quality route reads only cited records and the article delta. Shared evidence never supplies volatile claims, account state, platform selection, or `MODEL_TRANSLATION_FALLBACK`. A changed shared-pack hash does not silently amend a ready article: only citing articles rerun the applicable WQ author-QA or WR delta/full review.

### Mechanical preflight and observation

Immediately before WQ final QA or WR `FULL_REVIEW`, W runs `harnessctl.py check-review-ready`. It may reject stale contract/index bindings, absent or hash-drifted artifacts, malformed fixed payload, frozen CTA drift or unverified compiler companion; it does not issue an editorial, factual or SEO verdict. Without `COMPANION_DUAL_READ_REQUIRED`, HTML is the primary semantic surface; that explicit fallback requires dual reading in the applicable route. A failure returns to W for mechanical repair. Optional `summarize-efficiency` reads the completed task ledger and marks unavailable runtime metrics `UNAVAILABLE`; it does not estimate cost, create a role or gate release.

## Article-platform assignment contract

Before editorial dispatch for every current human-native release, freeze `article_platform_assignment.assignments[]`. It is a one-to-one mapping from every `article_id` to one owner-supplied `{platform, account}` in `platform_scope.allowed_pairs`. Article IDs, platform names and pairs must each be unique; a verified Blogger account, for example, is not a generic allocation pool. Missing/duplicate assignment is `ARTICLE_PLATFORM_ASSIGNMENT_BLOCKED_RECONFIRM_OWNER`. G may continue other fully mapped articles, but must not borrow, rotate, substitute or discover a platform; a remap needs fresh owner confirmation.

## Locale-platform compatibility contract

Freeze one `locale_platform_validation.rows[]` record per current article before W/R. A record contains its article ID, user-confirmed article language and market, mapped platform/account, `primary_reader_languages`, `primary_reader_markets`, `primary_audience_evidence_path`, `transport_supported_content_languages`, `transport_evidence_path`, and a `fit_mode`. The article language directs research and writing. Transport support only decides whether the format can carry the language; it never proves the primary reader audience. The ordinary decision is `PRIMARY_AUDIENCE_MATCH`. If language or market needs an exception, require `CROSS_LANGUAGE_EXCEPTION_OWNER_CONFIRMED` with the same exact owner wording/confirmation ID as the selection receipt; otherwise report `PLATFORM_AUDIENCE_MISMATCH_RECONFIRM_OWNER`. Do not translate the article or automatically reassign its platform.

## Title quality and field-mapping contract

Freeze focus keyword/approved variants, reader task, canonical article title, explicit platform title and SEO title per article. WQ's author-QA or WR's independent R judges semantic quality from topic clarity, reader intent, distinct value, natural language and market fit; G only verifies owner-specified title/SEO task fidelity. Literal keyword coverage and language-appropriate length are advisory, never standalone failure tests. Default `platform_title = canonical_title`; a platform field must not receive a shorter substitute while a stronger title exists elsewhere. A keyword-only, generic, misleading or task-mismatched title is `SEO-TITLE-INTENT-NNN`; an unapproved mapping divergence is `TITLE-FIELD-MAP-NNN`. Repair linked title fields, fingerprint and release card together, then rerun the applicable quality route.

## Content-first recommendation contract

The primary outcome of every article is an evidence-backed, standalone answer to its frozen reader task. A campaign CTA is required in every new schema-2.2 article, but never a completion quota or substitute for instruction, analysis, examples, evidence, or a balanced product comparison. It remains a secondary, contextually relevant recommendation. An explicitly requested product review or comparison may have a product-specific reader task, but it must still answer that task independently rather than becoming sales copy.

For the required secondary CTA, freeze a non-empty `reader_value_promise` and `cta` declaration: exact visible `anchor_text`, product identity, current destination, product/claim evidence path, reader-task relevance and exact relationship disclosure or `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Current schema-2.14 `article-package.json@1.5` carries frozen declarations and one-way upstream pointers. Mechanical package and payload checks verify the exact visible anchor, href and applicable disclosure without judging prose. W traces product claims to evidence and never invents reliability, rankings, tests, availability, outcomes or independent endorsement. In WQ, W adversarially checks whether the article remains useful without the CTA and whether the recommendation is secondary, relevant, evidence-bounded and transparent; in WR, independent R performs this judgment. G checks only declaration-to-delivery fidelity, not product quality or commercial prose. Schema-2.13/1.5 and earlier retain their own independent-R historical contract.

This policy reduces obvious promotional-spam and disclosure risk; it does not promise that a platform will retain a public page or replace current platform policies.

### Current schema-2.14 main-session execution

The default is `CONTINUOUS_CAMPAIGN_MAIN_SESSION` with `MAIN_SESSION_PATH_ISOLATED`. Each article has one disjoint root, `articles/<article_id>/`, containing `context`, `research`, `canonical`, `reviews` and `handoff`; its records stay within that root. Visible W sessions may be reused across ordinary WQ articles. WR activates a distinct visible R, independent of W, bound to that article's paths and records; shared G remains the campaign control plane. An idle R identity or W self-check never counts as an R review.

`GIT_WORKTREE` is an explicit exception only. Its `worktree_dispatch_decision` must record exactly one of `TRUE_CONCURRENT_WRITE`, `HIGH_RISK_REWRITE_OR_ROLLBACK` or `OWNER_REQUESTED_GIT_ISOLATION`. Token saving, an available slot or general capacity is never a reason to create a worktree; scheduling capacity only decides which safe, ready article proceeds next.

### Historical worktree-first behavior (schema-2.12 and earlier)

The owner pre-authorizes visible child-task delegation by default **after the pre-write confirmation passes**. Visibility, reusable role identity and Git worktree isolation are separate properties: a child task is not automatically a worktree. G records host capability before dispatch. When the project can create worktrees, campaign G creates one visible project-worktree task per ready independent article before the first W turn, waits for a usable worktree/task identifier, and records `GIT_WORKTREE`, task ID, path when exposed, base ref and lifecycle in `state.json.orchestration.article_workspaces[article_id]`. The worktree root is the article's reusable W/R pair, never a per-article G task. Its `role_bundle` records that W/R pair and the same registered `campaign_gatekeeper_agent` used by every article only for traceability. When worktrees are unavailable, campaign G records `WORKTREE_UNAVAILABLE` with the reason; a visible shared-workspace fallback is permitted only when article paths are disjoint, must be recorded as `VISIBLE_SHARED_WORKSPACE_WITH_PATH_ISOLATION`, and must never be represented as a worktree. Overlapping paths are `WORKTREE_ISOLATION_BLOCKED` and remain queued.

Campaign G is the visible controller and persistent **global owner-requirements gatekeeper**: after confirmation it creates `requirements-contract.md`, assigning stable `REQ-<AREA>-NNN` IDs only to explicit owner requirements. W maintains `requirements-traceability.md` per article, mapping each applicable REQ ID to canonical and final-payload evidence. R owns complete article quality—language, facts, research, SEO, title strategy, localization, structure, images and payload fidelity. Missing, substituted, weakened or out-of-scope requirements are `REQUIREMENT-<AREA>-NNN`; article quality findings remain R's `SEO-*`, `CONTENT-*` and similar stable IDs. G must not repeat R's semantic SEO, source, image or payload analysis. Instead, it runs one `BATCH_GATE_ACCEPTANCE` task for the exact set of currently R-approved rows, verifying frozen declarations, artifact hashes, reviewer approval and REQ traceability. A row can be `HUMAN_RELEASE_READY` while another is `CHANGES_REQUIRED`; a not-yet-ready article never holds the batch. Each role turn has a record in `state.json.orchestration.tasks[]` with an `agent_id`, bounded input paths, expected and actual output paths, timestamps, status and result reference. The batch task also records its exact `article_ids`; its report has one independently hash-bound row per article. Campaign G fills currently available runtime capacity with ready isolated work, then provisions the next priority-ready article worktree whenever a slot becomes available. Keep one visible, campaign-scoped `CAMPAIGN_OPERATIONS_STEWARD` in `state.json.orchestration.service_agents.campaign_operations` and resume it for readiness evidence, session recovery and bounded technical investigation. Do not create phase-named one-shot registration, preflight or recovery agents. Track `available_slots`, `active_article_pairs`, `active_article_worktrees`, `spawn_policy` and `last_dispatch_at` in `state.json.orchestration.capacity`. A saved report without that task record is not an independent-role artifact.

For each `article_id`, create exactly one visible `ARTICLE_WRITER` and `ARTICLE_LANGUAGE_REVIEWER`; reuse those identities only for that article's drafting, repair, full R and R-Δ. This preserves article language, brand and finding continuity without allowing cross-article context bleed. The reviewer remains independent of its article W; an ordinary bounded review uses the article contract, review index, cited baseline and changed evidence, while a full reread is required only under the defined rehydration/escalation triggers. If replacement is unavoidable, record `ARTICLE_AGENT_REPLACED`, article ID, old/new IDs and reason, then require `ARTICLE_AGENT_CONTEXT_REHYDRATED` before the replacement reports. Never leave duplicate active writers or reviewers for the same article; queue whole article W/R pairs only after all currently available runtime slots have been filled. After a human return receipt names `HUMAN_ACCEPTED`, the registered campaign G may include that article in its next `PUBLIC_QA_BATCH_READONLY` task. The batch report records a separate receipt/snapshot/visual-evidence-bound result per article; R is not resumed, W is not invoked, and no fresh public gate is created.

CLI is reserved for deterministic local mechanics such as file reads, JSON/schema checks, hashes, bundled scripts and version-control operations. Detached shell jobs, hidden CLI conversations and CLI-launched agents must not stand in for an independent role, editorial verdict, probe or experimental evidence. If the runtime cannot create a visible child agent, record `VISIBLE_DELEGATION_UNAVAILABLE`, preserve the resume point and block that stage rather than fabricate an independent result.

## Finding contract

Use `AREA-RULE-NNN`, for example `SEO-HEADING-001` or `CONTENT-SOURCE-002`. Preserve an ID across wording, line or recommendation changes. Each report declares `OPEN`, `RESOLVED` or `SUPERSEDED`; the review index preserves the same lineage without minting replacement IDs. An approved review and a PASS gate contain no open quality finding.

## State contract

`prewrite_planning → awaiting_owner_prewrite_confirmation → research → write → review → repair → gate → human_release_ready → human_publishing → human_accepted → public_readonly_validating → completed`

Allowed terminal alternatives are `deferred`, `blocked` and `capacity_blocked`. `human_release_ready` is an authorization for a human hand-off, never an instruction to publish automatically.

For current `AUTHOR_QA_INTEGRATED`, `research → write → author_qa` is W's continuous sequence, not an R approval boundary. `AUTHOR_QA_READY` is a distinct ready state, never independent approval. `INDEPENDENT_R_ESCALATION` adds independent R at full review; the separate early research-review record appears only when explicitly warranted inside WR. Historical `STANDARD_INTEGRATED_REVIEW` / `ELEVATED_EARLY_CHALLENGE` retain their independent-R semantics.

## Public reader-page contract

The public check is a narrow, read-only transport-and-contract comparison, not another editorial review. The human first saves `handoff/public-return-receipt.json`: public URL, exact `HUMAN_ACCEPTED` or `HUMAN_NEEDS_FIX` state, return time, known publication revision/timestamp or `UNVERIFIED`, optional rendered-page evidence paths, and any known limitation. Validate it with `harnessctl.py check-public-return-receipt`, then write a bounded `evidence/public-qa/public-snapshot.json`. The snapshot records only normalized public observations—fetch/result metadata, page title, public-text fingerprint, link/image counts and records, required CTA anchor/href/disclosure observations, and snapshot hash. It is a comparison aid, not a new editorial artifact or a publishing operation.

Only after `HUMAN_ACCEPTED` does the registered `CAMPAIGN_GATEKEEPER` consume the currently returned receipt/snapshot set in one `PUBLIC_QA_BATCH_READONLY` task and make the reader-page comparison against each most recently accepted canonical article and visual payload: page title, **rendered visual hierarchy** of sections/subsections, reader-visible body, declared links, image presence and observable Alt/caption, plus the required CTA's exact visible anchor text, href and applicable disclosure. It writes one batch report and one per-article `state.json.publication.articles[article_id]` record. Every record binds its own receipt's timezone-aware return time, local receipt/snapshot paths, registered campaign-G ID, batch/entry ID and hash, attempt and bounded `unverified_retry_count`; the files must exist and bind the same article/URL. The visible task row is `workflow_stage: PUBLIC_QA_BATCH_READONLY` with the same G ID, an exact article-ID set, timestamp and report path. It does not start R-P, invoke W, create a fresh public gate, or redo SEO/prose review.

| G result | Next action |
| --- | --- |
| `PUBLIC_QA_PASSED` | Mark that article complete. |
| `PUBLIC_QA_PASSED_WITH_LIMITATION` | Mark that article complete only when every reader-visible contract item still passes and the limitation is fully scoped. |
| `HUMAN_TRANSPORT_FIX_REQUIRED` | Write one consolidated human platform-side repair checklist; after repair, include only the affected article in the same G's next receipt/snapshot batch. Do not invoke W/R. |
| `PUBLIC_QA_UNVERIFIED` | Preserve URL/evidence and allow at most the policy's one bounded same-G recapture/recheck. Do not infer a pass or invoke W/R. |
| `CANONICAL_CHANGE_REQUESTED` | Requires an explicit owner request ID naming a canonical/payload change. Only then re-enter that article's applicable WQ author-QA or WR independent-R route. |

On a mismatch, the same G opens a stable `PUBLIC-CONTRACT-NNN` finding (or `PUBLIC-VISUAL-HIERARCHY-NNN` for visual hierarchy). A known or accepted platform limitation must name platform, account/site, editor/theme, locale/market, observation date, evidence paths and affected contract items, set `not_generalizable: true`, and state its owner-acceptance status. It can never become a universal claim about a platform. A limitation that fails a reader-visible contract item is `HUMAN_TRANSPORT_FIX_REQUIRED` or owner decision territory, not a passing limitation.

## Title transfer and visual hierarchy contract

Default `SEPARATE_TITLE_FIELD` tells the human to copy the title and body separately. `TITLE_IN_BODY` is for a target without a distinct title field. A hand-off manifest states this local selection mode, but it is not evidence of an editor or public-page HTML structure; the legacy `BODY_H1_REQUIRED` value is input-compatible only and normalizes to `TITLE_IN_BODY`.

The canonical outline and visual-payload tags are authoring aids. **Only the rendered public reader page decides heading hierarchy.** After `HUMAN_ACCEPTED`, campaign G uses visual reader-page evidence in the article's batch entry to check that the title, section headings and subsection headings are visibly distinct, ordered and not visibly duplicated or flattened. The normalized public snapshot may record whether visual evidence exists, but it cannot decide hierarchy. Editor HTML/DOM cannot be inspected, edited, requested from the human, or used as evidence. Public source/feed markup also cannot decide this hierarchy verdict. If rendered evidence is unavailable or inconclusive, write `PUBLIC_QA_UNVERIFIED`; do not infer success from the local payload.

## Cross-language SEO contract

When a campaign freezes cross-language SEO, W records dated target-region SERP semantic evidence and, when usable, optional global Google Trends context in one `research/evidence-pack.json`. Trends compares **English seeds and English long-tail candidates only** with query, global scope, window, date and capture path; relative-interest screens, `Breakout` or rising labels prove neither search volume nor low-base/high-momentum, local demand, commercial intent or model capability. Missing/inconclusive Trends never forces retries or blocks drafting. Select long-tail reader problem and wording from current brand site and target-region SERP, record evidence and uncertainty, and make no unsupported popularity claim. Platform profile may inform technical depth, examples, tone or transfer risk but cannot prove keywords, variants, intent or demand. Two independent no-consensus regional checks may support a recorded `MODEL_TRANSLATION_FALLBACK` with rationale and risk; never call it SERP-proven. WQ author-QA checks fluency, intent, evidence boundaries, declared `evidence_posture` and false claims; high-risk WR assigns those judgments to independent R. `METHOD_TEMPLATE_NO_EXECUTION` cannot claim A/B results, validation, stability, performance or causality; `DOCUMENTED_EMPIRICAL_RECORD` needs protocol, inputs/settings, run log, criteria and limitations. G checks only that owner-frozen localization tasks map to evidence and does not block solely on weak Trends.

When a current public brand-site variant serves the target locale and article intent, it is the highest-priority wording source: `CURRENT_BRAND_SITE → REGIONAL_SERP → MODEL_TRANSLATION_FALLBACK`. Record its URL, context and date; only a documented stale, misleading, unnatural or intent-conflicting condition permits a lower-priority choice. This maximizes message consistency and the brand-effect matrix.

## Scope contract

Only pairs in `campaign.json.platform_scope.allowed_pairs` may appear in a platform release package or state record. In current schema 2.14, empty scope, incomplete assignments, missing audience/transport records, or an unconfirmed cross-language exception blocks editorial dispatch because every article uses human-native release. No Skill can discover, append, substitute or reuse a pair. Schema 2.13 and older data retain their historical read/check contract and cannot bypass current dispatch rules.
