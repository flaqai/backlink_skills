# Editorial standard

Audit the canonical article before current-route quality binding: WQ author-QA or WR independent R.

| Surface | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Reader intent | Canonical article title, platform title and SEO title clearly serve the reader task, topic and market; assess their value and naturalness holistically, using literal keyword/length only as advisory signals. |
| Reader value | The article independently answers the reader task and remains coherent/useful without relying on the required CTA. A product-specific review or comparison remains evidence-backed and balanced. |
| Facts | Material claims have a source, cautious wording or removal. |
| Structure | One canonical article title; descriptive ordered sections/subsections; no empty headings or duplicated sections. Canonical markup is an authoring outline, not evidence of the target platform's final semantic tags. |
| Search language | Focus and supporting terms clarify the topic naturally; no density target. |
| Metadata | Title and description accurately summarize the article; platform title equals the canonical article title by default and no stronger SEO title is left unused. |
| Links | Descriptive text, real href, and an appropriate source or secondary-recommendation role. |
| Images | Rights/source, alt, caption and semantic position are recorded. |
| Recommendation integrity | The required frozen CTA is secondary and contextually relevant; its exact visible anchor text, product identity, destination and material claims have a traceable basis, any material relationship is clearly disclosed, and no unsupported reliability/ranking/test claim appears. |
| Voice | Direct, specific, non-templated and free of invented experience. |

Only record actual `FINDING` or material `WARN`; do not produce a green `PASS` checklist. Reproducible issues get stable IDs. WQ fixes and reruns its adversarial author-QA, yielding `AUTHOR_QA_READY` with final hashes or escalating to WR; WR returns to independent R with the appropriate full/delta review. G checks requirement fidelity and the route's truthful credential, not article semantics. Heading hierarchy is judged after `HUMAN_ACCEPTED` only from the rendered reader page; a public discrepancy does not reopen canonical content without an explicit owner correction request.
